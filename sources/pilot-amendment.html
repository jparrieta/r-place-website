Two durable boundaries

  1. Start: retain a verified package of the original session and coding/naming workspace before R1. Reuse an existing verified equivalent package rather than copying it again. Bind the source hash, native transcript/session, filesystem, exact runtime binary, installed instructions, model transition, tools and controller position. A binary hash without the retained executable is insufficient. Keep dependencies in an immutable shared bundle with a manifest; do not bundle credentials.
  2. Close: after the scientific checks pass, seal confidence against the model version, render/export deterministically, then compress and verify one final resumable package. It includes the session, transcripts, workspace, head, diary, critic packet/return, provenance and runtime bindings. Extraction and manifest verification must pass before declaring complete or reclaiming the working copy. The pilot also proves a closed copy can resume for interview without altering the sealed result.

No full snapshot at R1–R5, each turn, model pass or critic handoff. Live session persistence and journals continue; these are not advertised as immutable checkpoints. Do not delete old archives as part of this change.

Interruptions and technical faults

First inspect process identity and retain interrupted evidence. Where the native session and current filesystem remain consistent, continue that session in place. Reconcile outstanding tool effects before resuming. Do not generate a second reply to an already accepted tool or critic request.

If consistency cannot be established, hold that room with a concrete cause. Do not automatically roll back to the start, replay completed stages or substitute another agent. The start package is disaster recovery, with any lost-work replay declared and chosen explicitly. Fewer archives mean less rollback granularity; do not conceal this tradeoff.

Retry a failed render, export or archive operation after repair without another analytical turn. A recurring unchanged fault stays held. Repair wording/format failures in the file and rerun the affected check; do not reopen reading, critic work or pass counts for presentation alone. A changed claim, quote, episode or mechanism reopens its dependent obligations. Keep pinned global checkers unchanged. Scientific corrections go to the same lead; the two-failed-repairs limit counts classified scientific failures only, not technical faults or presentation repairs.

Persist one authoritative room state with separate analysis stage and technical status, the last operation, its result and next action. Preserve evidence without building another parallel queue. Accept a critic return once against its packet hash; corrections must not redispatch an accepted critic merely because closure failed.

Pilot and acceptance

Coder builds one immutable release in the external workspace. Record its source and installed amendment hashes. Reuse existing tests where they cover the behavior. Add only focused checks for: stage advancement without an archive; unchanged accepted critic reuse; interrupted-tool reconciliation without duplicate effects; deterministic close retry without a lead turn; final archive integrity; and resumed final interview against the stored session and files.

Retain prior results separately and do not show them to the new lead. Do not invent episodes or impose an episode quota. Keep one research lead active for this pilot. Do not resume broad batches or migrate a working world. Record analysis, support, waiting, validation and packaging durations separately, archive count and bytes, and research turns triggered by technical faults. Flag any transition validation above five minutes for diagnosis, not automatic failure or a bypass.

Success requires R6, sealed confidence, a verified archive and a closed-copy interview. Claude has offered the independent substantive review: after sealing, a reader who did not run the room traces claims to quotes/episodes and checks dynamics, counterevidence and explanatory abstraction. Keep the review outside the lead's workspace; do not feed it back into the sealed analysis. Record limits and uncertainty. Any later correction is a separate version retaining the original seal. Mechanical success alone is insufficient.

After each pilot, report measured costs and defects; verify closure, interview and substantive review and resolve actionable defects before the next. No concurrency expansion or old-queue refill is authorized. A targeted interruption test may use a disposable synthetic session; do not deliberately damage the research pilot.
