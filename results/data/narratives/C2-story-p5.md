# The story, pass 5 (C2d-02)

What these rooms did, told in the terms of the pass 5 model (`model/elements.jsonl`, `model/arrows.jsonl`, `model-p5.md`). Each sentence that uses an arrow names it in brackets after it. A sentence that tells an arrow's bound names the bound, "(A06's bound)", and is not a use of the arrow. A sentence that tells a claim names it and says it is untested for order. Hours are hours since the canvas opened. The earlier stories are `model/story-p1.md` to `model/story-p4.md`.

## Before the canvas

On 1 April a subreddit's readers are scattered. They share a topic, a team, a country or a joke, and nothing else. Many rooms spend the first day on April Fools: fake posts, takeovers, a licence to post what is not allowed on other days. The play runs beside the canvas work and does not, across rooms, hold it back. A room that comes late to the canvas comes late for its own reasons.

One room in nine has no posts in the week before the canvas opens. Among them are side subs that a readership opens for its canvas work, quiet subs the canvas wakes, and bodies the canvas gathers: a colour faction, or the makers of a work who form a room to speak for it. From their first post they run the steps below.

## Becoming able to act

Then one member starts. They propose a picture, map it in pixels, or begin placing it alone. The start gives the room something to answer, and calls for hands gather around it [A02]. Sometimes the call comes first and a member answers it with a proposal.

A call brings members to the canvas. They place, and they report how far the mark has come [A01]. Many answer the call in one line, "I'm in." or "On it.", which commits them and asks for nothing back [A12]. Those lines are how a room with no membership list sees its numbers.

The calls fill the host sub, and the sub draws a line around them. Moderators gather canvas talk into one thread, remove canvas posts under the sub's rules, or send members to a new canvas sub; members object to the flood or call the canvas a waste of time [A20]. While the canvas runs, both the moderators' rulings and the members' objections come after the calls. In some subs the rules remove the first call as it is posted, or a member opens a canvas sub before anyone calls. A member who judges the canvas a waste after the close answers no flood, and the line does not tell that judgment (A20's bound).

## Settling what to act on

What the room makes is mostly settled by the work and not by decision. A national room's picture is mostly its land's flag or emblems, and a fandom's is its characters; the room settles them as it settles any picture. Members argue a design in a thread, and someone settles it by posting a template that fixes it pixel by pixel [A11]. Sometimes the template comes with the first call and is never argued. The half-built mark shows the next placer what is missing. Few rooms elect a leader, take a vote or swear an oath.

## What followed: keeping the work

Once a mark stands on a surface anyone can change, keeping it becomes the work [A03]. Members watch it, repair changed pixels, cover the night and call for defenders when it is attacked. When attacks outrun what one pixel per interval can hold, members turn to scripts and spare accounts [A06]. Spare accounts can also build a first shape, or serve an attack, before the room has anything to keep (A06's bound).

## What followed: other rooms

Keeping the work brings the room up against its neighbours, and it talks with them [A04]. Borders are drawn: "go under", "shrink it horizontally", "draw around the black line". A settled border gives members a line they keep, and they spare the neighbour's work by it [A05]. Much sparing comes earlier and needs no talk.

A room short of hands turns to other rooms. It asks for help, "Maybe we should ask the Danganropa subreddit for support.", and other rooms send it [A13]. Talk over a border can open a channel for help: room for upkeep, pixels for protection [A14]. Under attack, rooms whose works face the same threat join in coalitions and defend each other [A19].

A room can also let another room's work live on its mark, or ask to live on another's. That is a claim on space, like a border. How it comes about is not yet told (below).

Some rooms meet other rooms before they have any work: a late room enters the canvas through another room's offer, or a room's neighbours are there from its first post. For them the talk comes first.

## What followed: the contest

A call to the canvas can also turn its hands against a rival's work [A15]. Some rooms' first canvas act is an attack on a rival they know. In some rooms the rival is named, in the same turn that calls the attack, by a tie the members had before the canvas: a league or school rivalry, a nation's history, an ideology, lore. "You mean WPI? I'm down for a lil interpolytechnic griefing." (C02, a claim, untested for order). Most calls to attack are answered inside the room. Members refuse them, rein the attackers in, apologise to the target [A16], or point to a truce already made [A17]. Where the attack goes on, it is carried on in talk: taunts, chants, claims of victory, and a salute when the fight is over [A18].

## What sends a room back

Some rooms lose. A neighbour overruns the mark, or an agreement breaks. A room that judges it has too few hands to hold its spot stops fighting and moves [A10]. A room inside a coalition can also move because the coalition plans it, and a late room may give its reason only after the move (A10's bound). The move sends it back to choosing a spot and cutting the design to what can survive. Its calls fall from the crisis before the move, but it keeps calling more than rooms that did not move, and it often calls again for the new spot. day9 lost its border at hour 48, moved within two hours, called members to a new plan, and finished the new work at hour 53. No loop is grounded.

## Hour 73

Placement stops at hour 73, and it does not come back. Calls to place, building, repair, scripts, attacks, help traded in pixels and border deals all end there. Whatever a room's work is at that hour is what it has.

## What they left behind

A mark reported done gives the room something finished to point at, and the room celebrates it together [A07]. Where the clock cut the work unfinished, the room marks the attempt: "We almost did it!" (A07's bound). What is celebrated is recorded: the final image, the timelapse, the list of what changed at the border [A08]. What is recorded is what a room carries on: the image into the banner, a plan for the next canvas, a sub kept for a new purpose, ties with other rooms [A09]. Some rooms plan the next canvas, or ask for the image in their flair, while the work is still kept (A09's bound). After the close a room can still celebrate, record, carry on, and keep a rivalry going in talk. It can no longer place.

## What the story does not tell

Membership carried in from outside the canvas is told only as the claim above, in the words of the turns that show it. Counted as an order, it does not come before the attack more than chance would put it. The story does not tell what membership did to any other step: why a room named one rival and not another, or how a sub's joke picked its work.

Two forms the model does not reach are boundaries, not steps the story leaves out.
- **Colour factions the canvas gathered.** Their faith names their rivals ("Blue the Purple Corner!"), rules on guests by vote and by fit with the colour, and splits the faction over doctrine: art on a coloured backdrop against a pure corner.
- **Hosts ruling on guests.** How a room comes to host other rooms' works on its mark is not told. Hosting follows neither help between rooms, a coalition, upkeep nor border talk. Hosts that give a ground for a ruling most often refuse on space or design; fewer admit a guest because its work fits their nation or colour. Hosts with readers before the canvas rule on guests too.

It does not tell what a room fears from the newcomers its mark draws, or how a room learns from others how to organize. Each is one room's residue.
