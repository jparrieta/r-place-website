# The story, pass 1 (C2a-01)

What these rooms did, told in the terms of the pass 1 model (`model/elements.jsonl`, `model/arrows.jsonl`). Each sentence that uses an arrow names it in brackets after it. Hours are hours since the canvas opened.

## Before the canvas

On 1 April a subreddit's readers are scattered. They share a topic, a team, a country or a joke, and nothing else. Many rooms spend the first day on April Fools: fake posts, takeovers, a licence to post what is not allowed on other days. In some rooms that play fills everything for a day or more, and the canvas does not come up at all.

## Becoming able to act

Then one member starts. They propose a picture, map it in pixels, or begin placing it alone. The start gives the room something to answer, and calls for hands gather around it [A02]. Sometimes the call comes first and a member answers it with a proposal.

A call brings members to the canvas. They place, and they report how far the mark has come [A01]. Many answer the call in one line, "I'm in." or "On it.", which commits them and asks for nothing back [A12]. Those lines are how a room with no membership list sees its numbers. Where a member had already begun, the call recruits to a mark that exists.

## Settling what to act on

What the room makes is mostly settled by the work and not by decision. Members argue a design in a thread, and someone settles it by posting a template that fixes it pixel by pixel [A11]. The half-built mark shows the next placer what is missing. Few rooms elect a leader, take a vote or swear an oath. Most never ask who is in charge.

## What followed: keeping the work

Once a mark stands on a surface anyone can change, keeping it becomes the work [A03]. Members watch it, repair changed pixels, cover the night and call for defenders when it is attacked. Upkeep brings the room up against the neighbours whose pixels erode its mark, and the room talks with them [A04]. Envoys carry proposals. Borders are drawn: "go under", "shrink it horizontally", "draw around the black line".

A settled border gives members a line they keep, and they spare the neighbour's work by it [A05]. Much sparing comes earlier and needs no talk: members refuse to cover a neighbour who left them alone. When attacks outrun what one pixel per interval can hold, members turn to scripts and spare accounts [A06].

## What sends a room back

Some rooms lose. A neighbour overruns the mark, or an agreement breaks. A room that judges it has too few hands to hold its spot stops fighting and moves [A10]. The move does not send it back to recruiting. It sends it back to choosing a spot and cutting the design to what can survive. In the threads after a move, new calls and new build reports are rarer than in the rest of the room's record. day9 lost its spot at hour 48, chose to move within two hours, and finished the new work at hour 53. The room did not return to the full cycle. No loop is grounded at pass 1.

## Hour 73

Placement stops at hour 73, and it does not come back. Calls to place, building, repair, scripts and border deals over pixels all end there. Whatever a room's work is at that hour is what it has.

## What they left behind

A mark reported done gives the room something finished to point at, and the room celebrates it together: "We did it!", credits to makers, members' own parts [A07]. What is celebrated is recorded: the final image, the timelapse, the list of what changed at the border [A08]. What is recorded is what a room carries on: the image into the banner, a plan for the next canvas, a sub kept for a new purpose, ties with other rooms [A09]. Most of this crosses hour 73. After the close a room can still celebrate, record and carry on. It can no longer place.

## What the story does not yet tell

The story does not say why some rooms came to the canvas late or never. The April Fools frame is the candidate and is untested. It does not say how rooms between which an attack ran organized as a pair, because attack and repair run between rooms. It does not say what membership carried in from outside the canvas, a nation or a colour tribe, did to any step.
