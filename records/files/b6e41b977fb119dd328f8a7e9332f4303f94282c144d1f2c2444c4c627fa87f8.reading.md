## The claim

**The literature left the model's spine standing.** It bounded one finding, supplied the mechanisms of two arrows, borrowed the concept's name, and located the model against the four universal problems.
- A member starts a mark, calls gather hands, the hands build, and keeping the mark becomes the work.
- A call is the first part of a pair. Joining is the answer it prefers and comes bare. Refusing an attack call is dispreferred and comes with a reason, and the reason states the room's norm or the attack's cost (F-014; conversation analysis).
- Decided order is rare, and gathers in the small canvas-born factions that govern a territory (F-006, bounded; O'Mahony and Ferraro 2007).
- The rooms handle all four universal problems in public talk, but by their own means: allocation by self-selection to calls, division by a template, information through the half-built picture and the chats, and reward as credit after the work. Nineteen of the 28 fourth-order themes fall outside the claim (F-016). Eight of them make the boundary and the goal that the claim assumes, and seven hold the order between rooms.
- The goal is made on the clock. Mostly it is a sign the room already had, proposed by one member and argued over in its pixels. Votes are rare, and loss changes the goal (F-017).

**The room that best supports it: day9.** It is a within-case room, told at passes 2 to 5. It builds from a call, loses its border at hour 48, moves within two hours, and finishes the new work at hour 53. Its goal changes after a loss, as F-017 counts for 116 rooms.

**The room that most threatens it: thebluecorner.** It is a colour faction whose faith names its rivals. It rules on guests by vote and by fit with blue, and it splits over doctrine. Stage C3 placed it: its setting is the nearest the canvas comes to O'Mahony and Ferraro's, a body governing a territory for others. That is where decided order gathers.

**The condition that separates them** is what decides whom a room fights and whom it admits. Where the work decides, the model tells the room. Where a body's faith or a host's rule picks rivals and guests, the model does not reach it, and the literature says why: there a basis of authority is being built.

## Works that contradict the model, and how each resolved (C3a)

- **O'Mahony and Ferraro (2007), verified: a difference of setting, bounding both claims.** An abductive read found decided order in 7 of 39 rooms placed on a colour-faction theme or on vetting guests, against 39 of 666 others (p 0.014 at held size). F-006 was bounded. Their "must" holds for governing a lasting body, not for organizing one work in hours.
- **Bechky (2006), verified: a difference of setting, bounding hers.** No careers carry roles between rooms. The model is one answer to her own open question (p. 17).
- **Felin and Zenger (2016; 2017), verified: about different things.** Their theory is a contrarian belief held against a market. A room's start is mostly a sign it already shares. The goal question went to C3c (F-017).
- **Olson (1965), recalled: open.** Members who never contributed are invisible in the record. Placement counts per account against each room's readership would decide it, and the study does not use the canvas as a ground.

## The four universal problems: the mapping and the remainder

The four universal problems claim, put to the structure as it stood, with nothing revised to fit it (`mapping-C3.jsonl`; F-016):
- **Information provision:** 4 themes (Sensemaking, Stigmergy, Backstage decision-making, Attribution), 555 rooms. Reached richly.
- **Reward provision:** 2 themes (Completion ritual, by construction; Visibility returns), 543 rooms. Reached richly as credit after the work, thinly as an inducement before it (9 rooms). The frame would cut through Completion ritual.
- **Task allocation:** 2 themes (Mobilization, by construction; Decided order), 394 rooms. Reached richly as self-selection (calls in 345 rooms), thinly as assignment (46).
- **Task division:** 1 theme (Standardization of output), 237 rooms. Reached only by an artifact; division among teams appears in 5 rooms.
- **The remainder: 19 themes**, covering talk in 694 rooms.
  - 7 between rooms, a change of level;
  - 8 that make the boundary and the goal, the frame's premises;
  - 1 different mechanism (machine hands under the rate limit);
  - the work itself (repair);
  - 2 after the clock.

**The verdict.** The material supports a weak form of the claim and is silent on necessity. On reward the claim cannot lose, since it allows intrinsic inducements and only "if its constituent agents need to be motivated to contribute" (p. 165). The material leans against sufficiency for telling forms apart, which stage D4 can test. For necessity, the structure does not speak to the claim.
