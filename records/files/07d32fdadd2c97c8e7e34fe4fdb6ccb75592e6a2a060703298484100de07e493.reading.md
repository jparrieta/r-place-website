# Implications (C5c-13; C5d's work redone after the serial work)

The model is the one that stands after pass 15: 23 standing elements, 20 drawn arrows and nine propositions (`account.md`, `figure-C5.md`, `propositions.jsonl`). The implications written at C5d, before the serial work, are kept as `implications-C5-p14.md`. The room, or a group of rooms, is the actor where its members spoke or acted for it.

## What the model implies for the theory question

The question asks:
- what forms organizing takes when strangers organize on a clock they did not set, with no way to compel anyone and no goal given to them;
- how the forms differ in their functioning;
- which of them keep the work alive.

- **One structure, run in different ways.** Rooms run one order: a start [R01], the room's calls for hands [P01], building [P03], and keeping the work [P05]. Each step follows the one before in most rooms carrying both (A02, 64 of 89; A01, 81 of 126; A03, 148 of 190). The forms differ in what the room meets after it builds:
  - neighbours, with whom it talks borders, trades help and joins coalitions [P08, P16, P17];
  - a rival, against whom a call turns, and whose attack call the room's own members often refuse [P09, P07, P18];
  - a loss, after which the room judges its hands too few and either moves or gives the work up [K02, P10; F-019];
  - or nothing, as in a late room that builds in one thread and stops at the close.

  These are paths through one structure, not separate kinds of organization.
- **Two routes for many hands.** The room coordinates by posting the state of its work in the thread (Direct information sharing, Okhuysen and Bechky 2009, p. 474), by sharing a template that divides the picture into tasks, or by both. 237 rooms post the work's state and 237 share templates, but only 89 do both, about what chance gives (F-022). The routes are carried largely apart. That is a difference in functioning between rooms: information about the work, or a division of the task laid out ahead of it.
- **Alignment without decision.** Rooms aligned many hands without deciding how they were organized. In 233 of the 370 rooms that called for hands, the room's record reports a work up after its first call (F-021); in the others, the room tried. A leader, a vote or an oath is coded in only 46 of 705 rooms (C01, F-006). Decided order is coded more often where a room governs a territory for a colour or vets others' requests to build.
- **How a room settles what to act on.** A call proposes what the room will act on. Most calls get no written reply: a member can accept by placing and write nothing (F-023). In written replies, acceptances come bare (25 of 31 to calls to place) and refusals give a reason (3 of 3 there; 23 of 38 to attack calls, F-014). A refusal contests the room's goal, and it is said with a ground: the room's norm, the attack's cost, or a prior claim on the space. Refusing a call to place is rarely written. Refusing an attack call is common.
- **Agreements no one can enforce.** Rooms make agreements with neighbours, and no one can make every placer keep them. Help between rooms often runs through a deal: where border talk comes first, the first help names a deal or the neighbour in 63 of 127 rooms (F-020). A room spares a neighbour's work mostly on its own norm, not on a settled line (F-020).
- **The clock closes making, not the room.** Placement stopped at hour 73 for every room. After it, the room records and curates the work and decides what to keep [P12, P13]. It can no longer place.
- **What the model does not tell.** The condition that separates the rooms the model tells from those it does not is what decides whom a room fights and whom it admits.
  - Where the work decides, through neighbours and erosion, the model tells the room.
  - Where a faith in a colour or a host's rule decides, the model does not (thebluecorner, catalunya).

## A counterfactual at a leaf

- **day9 borrows from ubc.** day9 offered a neighbour terms before any damage, lost the agreement at hour 48, judged its spot lost and moved [K02, P10]. ubc, a neighbouring leaf, kept a border by agreement [ubc/c0076].
  - *What to borrow:* A14's channel. A kept border opens a channel through which two rooms trade help, and that mechanism shows in the members' words (F-020). Had day9's agreement held, the model expects help to have come through it, and the move at hour 49 to have been less likely.
  - *What it cannot borrow:* A05's line, by which a settled border would make the neighbour's placers spare day9's work. That mechanism is theorised. Sparing rests mostly on each room's own norm (F-020), so a kept agreement would have held only as far as the neighbour's members chose.
  - *The limit:* the counterfactual borrows a mechanism; it does not predict an outcome.
- **ultrahardcore borrows from the spine.** ultrahardcore built late, in one thread, with no upkeep and no border talk. Had its mark stood earlier, A03 and A04 say the room would have turned to keeping it, and met the neighbours whose pixels eroded it. It would then have carried the talk with neighbours that its record lacks.

## Transferability, and where the model should fail

**The conditions, from the evidence:**
- **Voluntary participation.** Every placement was voluntary. No one held authority over another's placing (a setting fact). A call could ask for a member's time but not order it, and most calls drew no written answer (F-023).
- **Existing authority.** No one held authority over placing. Moderators held authority over their own threads, and they drew a line around canvas talk in 274 rooms [P20]. Decided order is coded in 46 of 705 rooms.
- **Resource access.** Each account could place one pixel per interval. Members in 236 rooms used scripts or spare accounts to place more [P14].
- **Commitments.** The record holds informal agreements between rooms that no one could enforce [P08], and help traded through them (F-020). It holds no legal commitment.
- **The external clock.** Placement stopped at hour 73 for every room. The site set the clock, not any room.
- **Whether failure dissolves a group.** It did not dissolve the room. 627 of 705 rooms had posts in the week before the canvas, and losing a mark did not end a room. It could end the organizing: after doubt and a loss, 11 rooms gave the work up (F-019). For the 78 rooms with no posts that week, the record does not show when they began.

**Where the conditions plausibly hold, and what the model predicts there.**
- *The settings:* shared surfaces open to all, with a rate per person and a fixed end, such as later pixel canvases, editing sprints with a deadline, and hackathons with no managers.
- *The prediction:*
  - the same order from a start to keeping;
  - calls answered by doing or in a bare line, and refused with a reason;
  - two routes, posted state and templates;
  - unenforced agreements with neighbours;
  - curation after the end.

**Where they do not hold, and what the model predicts there:**
- **Organizations with authority to assign work:** a decided order replaces the bare answer and the call.
- **Paid crowds with task allocation:** allocation replaces self-selection to calls.
- **Open-source projects with maintainers who lay out the tasks:** members' posts of the work's state are read within a structure someone keeps, so Direct information sharing would sit inside planning, as Warglien and Sartoris find for stigmergy in such settings (Moffett et al. 2021, p. 63).
- **Settings with no fixed end:** curation and carrying on are not tied to a terminus.
- **Settings where failure dissolves the group:** a loss ends the group, not only the organizing, and a move does not follow.

**What this record cannot check:**
- Talk in chats, which appears only where a member reports it in a thread.
- Placers who never wrote in the room, and acceptances made by placing.
- Replies that left no coded text: a fifth to a quarter of written replies (F-023).
- What survived on the canvas, which is not evidence here (rule 7).

**The frame.**
- 705 rooms are coded in this snapshot of 4 October 2026. 706 is the target census excluding r/place, and r/the_donald is not coded.
- Stage A1 dropped no room (`dropped.jsonl` is written and empty). Two leads outside the frame are noted in `leads-outside-frame-A1.jsonl`.
- The frame is built from artworks, so it cannot warrant claims about all communities that organized.

**Where the model should hold and break, in its own terms.**
- **It should hold** where the work decides whom a room meets: neighbours met through erosion, rivals met through a call.
- **It should break:**
  - where a faith in a colour picks the rivals and rules on guests (Neo-tribes, which no element uses);
  - where a host rules on guests on its mark (P19, a candidate);
  - where late rooms enter the canvas through another room (A04's and A19's bounds);
  - where the coders coded few of a room's replies.

## Expectations for the return (D1 to D3)

The return should find the following if the model composes. Stage D1 writes `EXPECTATIONS.jsonl` from these lines. Each names the elements, the kinds of room where they should appear, and where they should be absent.

1. **The spine.** Expect R01 or P01 before P03, and P03 before P05, in most rooms with a mark of their own. Expect it absent where a member placed alone before any call (A01's bound), or where the room found its mark already placed (A03's bound).
2. **Neighbours after upkeep.** Expect P08 after P05 in rooms that kept a mark among neighbours. Expect border talk first in late rooms that came to a full canvas and asked for space. Expect that first border talk mostly to offer terms before any damage (two coders: 19 of 26 to 36 of 38; A04's bound).
3. **The contest answered inside the room.** Expect P07 after P09 in rooms that carry attack calls. Expect written refusals to give a reason. Expect a tie from before the canvas named in the attack turn in some rooms with a league, school or national rivalry (C02, untested for order).
4. **Loss: a move or giving up.** Expect K02 before P10 in rooms that moved after a loss. Expect rooms that voice doubt after a loss either to move or to give the work up, in about equal numbers (10 and 11 of 36; F-019). Expect the doubt absent before moves made by choice or by a coalition's plan (A10's bound and N08's boundary).
5. **Decided order.** Expect C01 absent in most rooms. Expect it more often in rooms that govern a territory for a colour or vet others' requests to build.
6. **The host's line.** Expect P20 after P01 in standing subs. Expect it weaker or absent in subs opened for the canvas.
7. **Machine hands.** Expect P14 after P05 where upkeep is under strain. Expect scripts before upkeep where a room scripted the building from the start (A06's bound).
8. **The close.** Expect P11 after P03, P12 after P11, and P13 after P12. Expect no placement after hour 73, and curation of the record near the close and after it.
9. **The April Fools frame.** Expect K01 beside the work in rooms that played April Fools, with no delay of the work, and members asking whether a post is meant.
10. **Two routes for many hands.** Expect posting the work's state (P03's reports) and sharing templates (P04) to be carried apart, each in many rooms without the other, about as often as chance gives (F-022).
11. **Help through deals; sparing on the room's norm.** Where border talk comes first, expect the first help between rooms to name a deal or the neighbour in about half the rooms. Expect it less often where help comes first. Expect the first sparing to state the room's own norm more often than a settled line (F-020).
12. **Written answers.** Expect most calls to carry no written reply. Among written replies, expect acceptances to come bare and refusals with a reason (F-023, F-014).
13. **Where the model should not compose.** Expect rooms of colour factions, and rooms whose host rules on guests, to compose with a residue: the faith or the host's rule picks rivals and guests, and no element does.

## Which forms keep the work alive, answered from the record

This is answered from the record, never from the canvas (`organizing_end-A1.jsonl`; `_analysis/c5d_kept.py`).

- **The rooms' own reports.** Of 705 rooms, the record says the work was kept in 347 (0.492), lost in 218 (0.309), and falls silent in 140 (0.199). Silence leaves the outcome unknown.
- **The comparison by element.** A room that writes more carries more elements (contract section 5), so the comparison is also made within the middle half of rooms by size. There, 0.554 of rooms say their work was kept. The share rises somewhat in rooms carrying:
  - upkeep [P05]: 0.597;
  - border talk [P08]: 0.590;
  - attack calls [P09]: 0.622;
  - the move [P10]: 0.618;
  - the contest in talk [P18]: 0.632;
  - decided order: 0.645, over 31 rooms.

  It falls in rooms that voice doubt about their hands [K02]: 0.510. After doubt and a loss, rooms that move send their organizing back to a start, and rooms that give the work up end it (F-019).
- **The clearest difference is silence.** Rooms carrying these elements fall silent about the outcome less often (0.06 to 0.13, against 0.148). A room that organizes more also says more about what became of its work.
- **The work reported up.** In 233 of the 370 rooms that called for hands, the record reports a work up after the first call. The share runs from 24 of 60 in the smallest quarter of rooms by size to 80 of 110 in the largest (F-021).
- **The timing of the last coordinating turn.** The median `organizing_end_hour` is 72.02 (696 rooms). The aftermath share of coordinating turns after hour 73 has a median of 0.000 and a mean of 0.020. Most rooms' last coordinating turn falls near the close. The last coordinating turn is a last observation, not a demonstrated end.
- **Whose work this is about.** This compares what a room's members say about their own work in its threads. It does not say what survived on the room's patch of the canvas, since much of a room's artwork was placed by people who never wrote in the room. It is a comparison, not a prediction, and stage D5 reads it against the canvas by branch.
- **What the model implies for which forms keep the work alive.** The rooms that meet neighbours and rivals and keep the work report it kept a little more often than others. No form in this record sorts kept from lost by much. The model explains how rooms organized better than whether their work survived.
