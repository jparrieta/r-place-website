## The method question, in one sentence

Can independently reconstructed case histories be composed into a shared dynamic account that supports explanations at successive levels of specificity, from population-wide patterns, through forms and subforms, to particular organizational histories, while keeping access to the evidence from which those explanations were developed?

The theory question is unchanged: when strangers organize on a clock they did not set, with no way to compel anyone and no goal given to them, what forms does organizing take, how do they differ in their functioning, and which of them keep the work alive?

## How the B1 walk went, and what carries into B1c and B1d (the stage file, kept here for after a compaction)

- **B1d.** Write the chosen rooms to `out/pass2-chosen-B1.txt` first (the rooms B1c finds not fitting, the rooms with the most hesitations, the contrary rooms); read them in the order, concepts and forests, recording each with `mem.py reread <sub>`; stop only when every chosen room is read and the run since the pass's last new kind reaches marker 1's empty run, never fewer than thirty rooms (`saturation.py pass2 --stage B1`).
- **Startup packet each leg:** this head, the stage file, the last five diary entries, `mem.py scheme --brief` (paged at 80 lines: `--offset 80`, 160), `validate.py --open B1`.
- **Per room.** Read the A1 note (`mem.py note`) and every theme's note and concept labels; the room on its own first, then against read rooms tied to it. An episode needs both records to tell it, by one thread or one artwork in the same hours; a member writing in both rooms is strong evidence. One member carrying the same call into several rooms counts (E249). Mark "same": "thread" only when both rooms' codes sit in that thread. A member's name only from `cite.py`; quote a disputed or political code through `cite.py` before describing it.
