D2 · Adaptation: labelers and verdicts

Collect the communities' answers

D2 brings back two kinds of answer: each community coder's account of its community in the model's terms, and independent readers' judgments of how well the model fits. It checks that the answers arrived intact before anyone interprets them.

Where this stage starts

D1 has prepared and fixed the tasks. The adaptation handler resumes each community's own community coder, with its original conversation and files. Separate readers receive the verdict packets. The aggregator's check leg opens after these jobs have returned their files.

What the community coders, readers and aggregator do

Each community coder rewrites its local account in the population model's terms. It records where the model applies, where it needs detail or a condition, where the community contradicts it, and where a new idea may be needed. Each claim cites evidence from that community. A community that needs no adaptation still submits an explicit answer; silence counts as a missing return.

Two independent readers judge whether the model accounts for the community, accounts for it with something left over, fails to account for it, or cannot be judged from the evidence. Their reasons and disagreements stay visible. The community coder's own assessment sits beside these verdicts, and each is kept.

The ingestion tools check model versions, identifiers, citations, required fields and completeness. The handler can send a failed return back for repair. The aggregator then reads the ingestion report and a seeded sample of ten local accounts: do they describe how those communities organized, or only repeat the population model?

These checks show whether a return can be read and traced. Whether a proposed correction holds is decided in D3. A high disagreement rate or many exceptions is reported as found, and no answer is adjusted to improve the numbers.

What the next stage receives

D3 receives the accepted adaptation records, both verdicts, the local accounts and the explicit gaps. D3 judges the substance of the corrections; D2 only collects them.

What the output files contain

Open the saved files from stage D2.

Markdown files hold prose; JSONL files hold one structured record per line.

The adaptation ledgeradaptations.jsonl

adaptations.jsonl collects the validated return lines with their evidence and model version. "Validated" means the required structural checks passed; the aggregator rules on each claim in D3.

The independent judgmentsverdicts-D.jsonl

verdicts-D.jsonl keeps the readers' fit judgments and reasons beside the community coders' assessments. Each reading stays visible, with no vote between them.

The local accounts and comparison tablerooms/<sub>/model-D.md, verdict-D, _views/rooms-x-elements.tsv

rooms/<sub>/model-D.md holds each returned local account, with its verdict-D record beside it. _views/rooms-x-elements.tsv sets communities against model elements, so D3 can read all the evidence on one claim together.

What arrived, failed or remains missingingest-D.md, notes-D2.md, RUN.json, run_manifest.json, handback-D2.md

ingest-D.md reports accepted lines, failed rules and missing communities. notes-D2.md records the sample checks and cautions on substance. RUN.json records return counts; run_manifest.json records the models used. handback-D2.md carries defects and open operational questions forward.

Historical instructions and settings

Study-specific settings: the 15 October decision specified a 5.6 resume followed by a 6.1 return. Native D2 turns checked for all 14 shared communities use gpt-6.1-sol, medium. Their run.json files keep resumed_with=5.6, a field that does not name the model of those answer turns. On 21 October the D3 re-check was assigned gpt-6.1-sol, medium. Its return records name 6.1 and the same D2 sessions, but omit effort. This website review did not inspect every D3 native turn or all 705 D2 histories.
