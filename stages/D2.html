D2 · Adaptation: labelers and verdicts

Collect the communities’ answers

D2 brings back two kinds of answer: each original lead’s account of its community in the model’s terms, and independent readers’ judgments about how well the model fits. It checks that those answers arrived intact before interpreting their implications.

Where this stage starts

D1 has prepared and fixed the tasks. The adaptation handler resumes each matching community lead with its original conversation and files. Separate readers receive the verdict packets. The aggregator’s check leg opens after these jobs have returned their files.

What the leads, readers and analyst do

Each community lead rewrites its local account using the population model. It records where the model applies, where it needs detail or a condition, where the community contradicts it, and where a new idea may be needed. Each claim needs evidence from that community. A community needing no adaptation still submits an explicit answer; silence is a missing return.

Two independent readers also judge whether the model accounts for the community, accounts for it with something left over, fails to account for it, or cannot be judged from the evidence. Their reasons and disagreements remain visible. The lead’s own assessment sits beside these verdicts and does not replace them.

The ingestion tools check model versions, identifiers, citations, required fields and completeness. The handler can return a failed response for repair. The analyst then inspects the report and a seeded sample of ten local accounts: do they describe how those communities organized, or merely repeat the population model?

These checks establish whether a return can be read and traced. They do not decide that a proposed correction is scientifically warranted. A high disagreement rate or a large number of exceptions is something to report, not a reason to adjust answers until the numbers look better.

What the next stage receives

D3 receives the accepted adaptation records, both verdicts, local accounts and explicit gaps. It can now assess the substance of corrections. D2 leaves that adjudication to D3 rather than mixing collection with judgment.

What the output files contain

Open a file group for its purpose. These are descriptions of the stage’s outputs. Markdown files hold prose; JSONL files hold one structured record per line.

The adaptation ledgeradaptations.jsonl

adaptations.jsonl collects validated return lines with their evidence and model version. “Validated” here means that the required structural checks passed, not that the analyst has accepted the claim.

The independent judgmentsverdicts-D.jsonl

verdicts-D.jsonl preserves the readers’ fit judgments and reasons beside the leads’ assessments. It makes disagreement visible instead of reducing two readings to a vote.

The local accounts and comparison tablerooms/<sub>/model-D.md, verdict-D, _views/rooms-x-elements.tsv

rooms/<sub>/model-D.md holds each returned local account, with its verdict-D record alongside. _views/rooms-x-elements.tsv arranges communities against model elements so D3 can inspect all evidence concerning one claim together.

What arrived, failed or remains missingingest-D.md, notes-D2.md, RUN.json, run_manifest.json, handback-D2.md

ingest-D.md reports accepted lines, failed rules and missing rooms. notes-D2.md records the sample checks and substantive cautions. RUN.json records return counts; run_manifest.json records the models used. handback-D2.md carries defects and unresolved operational matters forward.
