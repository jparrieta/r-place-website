Overview/The instrument/C1 · Model: structuring

C1 · Model: structuring

Turn the categories into a structure

C1 asks whether the coding scheme still makes sense after use across the collection. It repairs documented weaknesses, then begins grouping descriptive categories into broader ideas about organizing.

Where this stage starts

B4 supplies settled placements and their disagreements. The aggregator also has the pilot results, categories that readers often combined, and themes left unplaced. This provides evidence about where the scheme worked and where its boundaries obscured the material.

What the aggregator does

The aggregator revisits troublesome categories and a seeded sample of placements. It can split a category that combines different actions, merge categories that lack a useful distinction, add a missing category, or retire one that does no work. Each change needs a reason and a record of the affected material. A split requires rereading: the old label alone cannot determine which new category an item belongs to.

Multiple placements need particular care. An act may genuinely perform two functions, or the categories may be poorly separated. Reader confidence does not settle which explanation is right. Nor should the scheme be changed merely to make readers agree with its author.

The aggregator also returns directly to concepts and participant language. It looks at how turns are formed and sequenced, how ways of speaking vary, and what participants appear to take for granted. This pass can reveal patterns hidden by earlier labels.

Finally, C1 groups the descriptive, third-order themes into fourth-order themes: broader constructs that explain what several categories have in common. These groups need an argument and boundaries, not just a larger label. Their number is not fixed in advance. The stage also distinguishes ideas borrowed from existing work from those adapted or named here.

What the next stage receives

C2 receives a revised coding structure and a complete, reconciled placement record. It can ask how these parts relate in an explanation of organizing. C1 supplies the parts and their grouping; it has not yet established a model of how they work together.

What the output files contain

Open a file group for its purpose. These are descriptions of the stage’s outputs. Markdown files hold prose; JSONL files hold one structured record per line.

The revised rulesscheme_v2.md, scheme_v2.jsonl

scheme_v2.md explains the revised scheme; scheme_v2.jsonl records it for tools. These are complete versions, so later stages need not reconstruct the rules from scattered edits.

Every placement, with the changes kept separateassignments_final.jsonl, assignments_final.count.txt, assign_revisions_only.jsonl

assignments_final.jsonl contains the full placement record. assignments_final.count.txt records its reconciled count. assign_revisions_only.jsonl lists just the changes, allowing a reviewer to see what C1 altered without mistaking that subset for the whole collection.

The broader themesfourth/fourth.jsonl, fourth.md

fourth/fourth.jsonl records the fourth-order groups and their members. fourth.md explains why those groups belong together and what they mean. Group membership alone is not an explanation.

What a direct reading of language revealedform_pass-C1.md

form_pass-C1.md records observations about turn structure, language and assumptions that the earlier category labels might conceal.

Reasons and the next steptier_history.jsonl, notes-C1.md, handback-C1.md

tier_history.jsonl records changes across levels of the scheme. notes-C1.md explains choices and unresolved issues. handback-C1.md tells the next stage what changed, what was checked and what still needs judgment.
