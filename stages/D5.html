D5 · Forming: branch accounts

Explain what each proposed form actually does

A cluster label is not yet an account of an organizational form. D5 returns to the member communities and asks how each group organizes, what its members share and where that explanation breaks down.

Where this stage starts

D4 has fixed branch definitions and cross-links. The aggregator has the local community accounts, their adaptations, the population model and its limits. It can inspect the records behind a branch, but it cannot redraw the branches or change the model here.

What the aggregator does

For each branch, the aggregator writes a narrative a stranger to the model could follow. It explains what the group shares with the population account, what it does differently, and which participant evidence supports those differences. Representative cases include a poor fit, so a neat label does not conceal variation.

The account then asks whether the proposed form links constructs through a defensible explanation. A group whose shared codes cannot be connected to how its members organized remains a grouping. Branch size alone does not make it important, and a name that fits the codes but not the communities needs criticism.

Comparisons of absence require care. If a relationship is rare throughout the collection, failing to observe it in a small branch may tell us little. The stage records whether there was enough opportunity to test the claim before treating an absence as a boundary condition.

It also keeps three judgments apart: whether the original population model fit, whether the revised local account is supported, and whether the shared branch explanation is supported. A failed original fit can coexist with an adequate later local account.

Finally, the aggregator follows one explanation down to a community and its quotations, then back up. It examines how long coordination remained visible and what the records say survived. Silence stays unknown; canvas remains do not by themselves prove that organizing continued.

What the next stage receives

D6 receives the branch accounts and their limits, ready to be woven into the whole model. Suggested renamings or new distinctions travel forward as proposals. If no shared branch is supported, the account proceeds directly from the global model to local evidence.

What the output files contain

Open a file group for its purpose. These are descriptions of the stage’s outputs. Markdown files hold prose; JSONL files hold one structured record per line.

The account of each branchforms/<id>-account.md

forms/<id>-account.md explains members’ organizing, shared changes, representative and difficult cases, nearby branches, and reasons to doubt the proposed form. It distinguishes evidence for common codes from evidence for what participants actually did.

A comparable record of branch judgmentsbranches-D5.jsonl

branches-D5.jsonl records branch properties, model versions, local uncertainties and the shared explanation’s status. Its structured format allows later checks without replacing the narrative argument.

A path from the model to a quotationwaterfall-D5.md

waterfall-D5.md walks through the global account, a branch where supported, a local account, its adaptations and source words. It states what each level adds and whether any step contradicts the one above.

What continued and what remainedsurvival-D5.md, findings.jsonl, defects.jsonl, notes-D5.md

survival-D5.md reports observed coordination, later discussion and evidence about work kept or lost. findings.jsonl records claims; defects.jsonl records problems. notes-D5.md carries unresolved issues and proposals into D6.
