Overview/The instrument/B2 · Coding: pilot

B2 · Coding: pilot

Test whether others can use the scheme

A category can seem clear to its author and confuse everyone else. B2 tests that problem on a small set before the scheme is used across the whole collection.

Where this stage starts

B1 has supplied provisional categories and rules for assigning material to them. B2 starts with those rules, known confusing pairs, and both difficult and randomly selected examples. “Placement” means assigning a local theme to one or more of the common categories.

What the analyst does

The author records their own answers before seeing anyone else’s. Fresh readers then receive the scheme and examples, without the author’s working notes or answer key. Three readers examine each pilot item. Their replies show where the rules lead to different interpretations.

The analyst asks why each important disagreement arose. Was a rule vague? Did one local theme bundle several kinds of action? Did a reader miss evidence? Was the author’s original answer wrong? These explanations matter more than making an agreement score rise. The scheme can be revised, and every revision must have a recorded reason.

The pilot also tests whether small changes in the wording of instructions alter placements and whether readers behave differently near the beginning and end of a batch. This helps determine how much material later readers should receive together.

Rounds stop under the declared rule: no further edit, the declared agreement threshold, or the four-round limit. Any remaining weakness stays in the report. A separate set of reference answers is then used to check the frozen scheme. These are the author’s judgments, sometimes called “gold” answers; they are not an external truth about the communities. Agreement measures consistent use of the instrument, not whether its interpretation is correct.

What the next stage receives

B3 receives one fixed version of the scheme, a reply format and evidence about batch design. Freezing the rules allows disagreements in the full reading to be compared under the same instructions. It does not make those rules immune to later criticism.

What the output files contain

Open a file group for its purpose. These are descriptions of the stage’s outputs. Markdown files hold prose; JSONL files hold one structured record per line.

The reference answersauthor_items.jsonl, gold_items.jsonl

author_items.jsonl records the author’s pilot placements before comparison. gold_items.jsonl holds the separate reference items and judgments used to assess the frozen scheme. Their role is a declared comparison standard.

The reader instructionsRETURN-B2.md

RETURN-B2.md defines what a reader must return. It makes replies comparable and lets the handler distinguish an incomplete reply from a substantive disagreement.

What each round changedpilot_rounds.jsonl, scheme_edits.jsonl, exemplar_candidates.jsonl

pilot_rounds.jsonl records the rounds and results. scheme_edits.jsonl links each rule change to its reason. exemplar_candidates.jsonl identifies difficult examples that might help readers in the full reading.

Stability and the overall assessmentstability-B2.md, pilot_report.md, notes-B2.md

stability-B2.md reports sensitivity to different wording. pilot_report.md brings together agreement, reference matching, remaining confusions and practical limits. notes-B2.md records settings, thresholds and decisions.

The frozen scheme and its provenancescheme_final.md, scheme_final.jsonl, run_manifest.json

scheme_final.md is the readable scheme; scheme_final.jsonl is the equivalent structured record used by tools. The stage’s run_manifest.json entry records how the pilot was run, so later results can be tied to the tested setup.

How the stage is divided into legs
  • B2a: build round. Repeats: round. Interview point: -.
  • B2b: check round. Repeats: round. Interview point: -.
  • B2c: gold and stability build. Repeats: no. Interview point: -.
  • B2d: gold and stability check. Repeats: no. Interview point: -.
