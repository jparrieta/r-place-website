D4 · Forming: the tree

Find shared forms in the changes communities need

D4 asks whether communities differ in patterned ways. Communities may need the same correction to the population model, add similar detail, or do the same work through different processes. Such shared adaptations can suggest organizational forms.

Where this stage starts

D3 has frozen the model and ruled on its adaptation records. Each record names what changes, how it changes and the supporting evidence. Similarity can be measured at several levels: a third-order theme, a fourth-order theme or an aggregate dimension.

What the aggregator does

The aggregator first inspects the recorded similarities and their limits. A dendrogram shows how cases join as dissimilarity grows. It pictures those differences and makes no claim about descent or about a tree-shaped structure in organizing. The population model is a conceptual reference point.

A candidate branch needs more than a convenient split in the picture. The aggregator names the shared changes, reads the supporting communities and explains the mechanism or condition that holds the group together. Changes that meet only at a broad conceptual level support a weaker grouping than the same specific correction.

A community can belong to more than one branch when different parts of its activity support different links. These cross-links are recorded, and no case is forced into a single box. Explicit "no adaptation" returns also stay apart from missing returns, unresolved claims and cases with only local particulars.

After the groups are fixed, a separate calculation groups communities by the composition of their coded themes. This comparison uses no adaptation records and no outcomes, but it shares the study's coding structure, so agreement between the two methods is weaker evidence than two independent tests. Verdicts and canvas measures are added afterwards, with checks for coverage, size and sensitivity.

What the next stage receives

D5 receives the branch definitions, memberships, cross-links, comparison results and an account of every community. It tests whether each group supports an account of organizing. Finding no supported shared forms is a valid result, and the procedure keeps no branch for the sake of a tidy tree.

What the output files contain

Open the saved files from stage D4.

Markdown files hold prose; JSONL files hold one structured record per line.

The visual tree and its definitions_views/adaptation-tree.html, .svg, .json, branches.jsonl, forms/<id>.md, <id>

_views/adaptation-tree.html, .svg and .json give interactive, graphic and structured views. branches.jsonl defines the accepted branches. forms/<id>.md gathers their members and supporting changes; <id> is the branch identifier.

Overlap and the reasons for cutscrosslinks.jsonl, tier_history.jsonl, tree-D4.md

crosslinks.jsonl records overlapping memberships. tier_history.jsonl records each cut and its grounds. tree-D4.md explains the chosen representation, its limits and the communities outside every branch.

The separate composition comparisonchunk-share-D4.md, chunk-share-D4.jsonl, chunk-share-D4.py

chunk-share-D4.md explains the results; chunk-share-D4.jsonl records memberships; chunk-share-D4.py is the entry point for replay. The linked result record keeps the numerical diagnostics. This comparison uses the composition of coded themes, and none of the adaptations that defined the branches.

What is added after groupingprojections-D4.jsonl, notes-D4.md, taxonomy-status.json

projections-D4.jsonl records verdict and canvas summaries for the branches and the comparison clusters. notes-D4.md explains choices and sensitivity. If no shared forms are supported, taxonomy-status.json records that result and its grounds.
