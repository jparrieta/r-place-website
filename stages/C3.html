Overview/The instrument/C3 · Model: literature

C3 · Model: literature

Put the model into conversation with theory

C3 asks what existing research helps explain, challenges or has already said about the model. Literature is a source of comparison and criticism, not a template into which every observation must fit.

Where this stage starts

C2 has built a model from the community material and tested its successive tellings against cases. The aggregator also has its initial expectations. Keeping that record makes it possible to distinguish ideas brought to the study from ideas developed during it.

What the aggregator does

The aggregator begins with literature that could contradict the model. When a published explanation differs, it asks what causes the difference: a different setting, a real disagreement about how organizing works, or different terms for similar processes. It names evidence that could distinguish these possibilities and leaves the question open when the record cannot decide.

Related work can then sharpen mechanisms and concepts. A borrowed idea must earn its place by explaining the material, not merely by giving the account a familiar theoretical name. The aggregator records which sources it actually opened and which it recalls but has not verified. A remembered citation cannot carry the same evidential weight as a checked source.

The stage also compares its broader themes with four organizing problems: dividing tasks, assigning them, providing rewards and handling information. That comparison helps a reader locate the model in organization theory. It does not require every theme to fit one of the four. Material left outside the framework may be part of what the study contributes.

Literature can prompt another look at a community or a change to the model. Those changes need an evidence trail. Earlier formulations remain available, and findings affected by a revision are reconsidered. The aggregator also returns to its original expectations and states what the analysis changed.

What the next stage receives

C4 receives the current model, its relationship to existing explanations, and unresolved disagreements. A version list makes clear which files are current, preventing a later stage from accidentally combining incompatible versions of the account.

What the output files contain

Open a file group for its purpose. These are descriptions of the stage’s outputs. Markdown files hold prose; JSONL files hold one structured record per line.

The comparison with literatureliterature-C3.md, findings.jsonl

literature-C3.md explains agreements, contradictions and conceptual links, with checked sources distinguished from unverified recollections. findings.jsonl connects relevant claims to that literature record.

The organizing-problem comparisonmapping-C3.jsonl

mapping-C3.jsonl maps broader themes to the four organizing problems where justified. It also preserves what does not fit, so the comparison does not silently narrow the model.

Changes to the explanationmodel/arrows.jsonl, model/history.jsonl, model-C3.md, structure-C3.md, concept-C3.md

model/arrows.jsonl records current relationships; model/history.jsonl records changes and reasons. When needed, model-C3.md, structure-C3.md and concept-C3.md provide complete successor accounts rather than disconnected patches.

Which version to readmanifest-C3.txt, notes-C3.md, tier_history.jsonl, scheme_v2.jsonl, assignments_final.jsonl

manifest-C3.txt identifies which accounts have successors and which earlier files still apply. notes-C3.md explains choices and remaining questions. If the coding structure changes, tier_history.jsonl, scheme_v2.jsonl and assignments_final.jsonl record the change and its consequences for placements.
