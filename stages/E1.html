E1 · Formalizing: audit

Audit what the run supports

E1 checks the completed analysis against its records. It separates what can be recomputed from what the aggregator remembers or believes, then judges whether the model links population claims to local evidence well enough.

Where this stage starts

The aggregator can inspect the outputs, logs, saved versions and handoffs of the whole run, including D6. Earlier files stay as they were. An error found now becomes a recorded defect or a new correction file, and the evidence under audit stays unchanged.

What the aggregator does

Scripts recompute counts, trace quotations and identifiers, and check whether the required steps happened in the prescribed order. A separate check that the handler supplies compares quoted material with the raw record. The audit reports that check apart from the resolution of citations within the coded store.

The aggregator examines how the model developed: which cases changed successive stories, what readers were given, which category revisions occurred, and which later claims use each stage's outputs. A file that was produced and never used may point to unnecessary work or a broken handoff.

It then opens the predictions sealed before the return to communities and compares them with the answers. The comparison stays within the sample, because those communities helped build the model. First global fit, current local adequacy and support for shared forms are reported separately, with versions and unresolved evidence.

For a seeded sample of thirty communities, fresh readers judge whether the population model plus the supported local adaptations can reconstruct the claims in the community account. A poor reconstruction can come from missing evidence, an omitted local detail, an unsupported claim or a weak shared model. The audit looks for the cause in each case, and no single score gives the verdict.

Finally, the aggregator writes its experience of the run against the audited record and says where it cannot remember. New D6 proposals stay marked as unvalidated.

What the next stage receives

E2 receives a reproducible audit, the study's limits and the aggregator's separate account of its experience. The judgment on composition may be supported, unsupported or unresolved, with its level and scope stated. E2 can then examine the instructions and handling that shaped the work.

What the output files contain

Open a file group for its purpose. These are descriptions of the stage's outputs. Markdown files hold prose; JSONL files hold one structured record per line.

The audit and reproducible calculationsaudit-E1.md, audit-E1/, claims-E1.md, probes-E1.md

audit-E1.md reports checks, limits and the account of methods. audit-E1/ holds scripts and outputs for replay. claims-E1.md checks whether each claim received the kind of evidence it needs: source words or a pattern across cases. probes-E1.md reports recall checks and separates forgotten information from questions the record could not answer.

Expectations checked against returnsexpectations-E1.md

expectations-E1.md compares the sealed predictions with the communities' answers, including claims that came from the Field account. It keeps an expected absence apart from something a case could not test.

The local reconstruction testreconstruction-sample-E1.jsonl, RETURN-E1.md, reconstruction-E1.jsonl

reconstruction-sample-E1.jsonl records the thirty selected communities before inspection. RETURN-E1.md specifies the reader task. reconstruction-E1.jsonl keeps the results and ties each to a particular claim.

Experience and the final handoffexperience-E1.md, notes-E1.md, handback-E1.md, run_manifest.json

experience-E1.md is the aggregator's first-person account, kept apart from computed evidence. notes-E1.md explains audit decisions. handback-E1.md carries problems and lessons forward. The last run_manifest.json entry records the experience file and the model that wrote it.
