How the aggregator remembers

Kind of page: reference.

The analyst holds nothing between legs except what is on disk: "the file, not your recollection, is what the run trusts" (the contract). Each part links to its file's page.

A fresh session per leg

Every leg is a new Claude session. step.sh writes the leg's first message and launch.sh starts claude with a new session id and auto memory switched off, "no memory carried between legs". What carries the work is the startup packet that AGENT.md and CLAUDE.md name: the head, the stage file, the last five diary entries, and validate.py --open NN. Nothing else is read by default.

The head

_head.md is the narrative of the argument, at most 3,000 words, read first at every leg and rewritten at every close and before every cut. done.sh keeps a leg open when the head runs over 3,200 words, and links.py fails the next launch when an id in it does not resolve.

The diary

The diary is the record under the head. It is written only through mem.py log, stamped with the time and the leg, in parts of 1,500 words. Each line has a kind: opened, dropped, merged, split, kept, move, abductive, question, comparison, note, helper, minted, placed, unplaced, pause, or one a stage file names. Rule 20: a diary written at the close from memory "is not a diary." A leg reads its tail or greps it by id; E1 computes over it.

Read logs and the verbs

Every call to mem.py and cite.py leaves a line in the read logs. Rule 1: where the analyst's account of its reading and the log disagree, the log is right. The verbs serve reading one page at a time, generated views and wiki pages included. The room notes written at A1 are the run's prior, queried one at a time and never read as a list.

The recall probe at close

After done.sh, close.sh asks five questions in a fork of the leg's session, with tools disallowed: the method question, the open distinctions, the last three rooms opened, what changed and why, the next comparison. probe.py scores the answers against the questions, the read log and the history files,; _probe/ keeps them. A miss the head or the diary answers is a note; a miss neither answers is a defect.

Snaps and checkpoints

snap.py hashes every file in out/ under a name, once. close.sh takes one at every close; each categorization move gets one too, so a reviewer can see the state before and after it (_snap/). close.sh then publishes a checkpoint of the folder and the transcript, committed once.

Compaction

A long leg will compact unseen. CLAUDE.md tells the compactor what to keep. The hook after_compact.sh then has the analyst re-read the head and the stage file, read the diary's tail, reconcile the two, and log that it did.

Pauses

pause.sh writes .pause. At the next safe stop the analyst logs its working hypothesis and the next room or item as a pause line and closes with done.sh NN --pause, which refuses without that line. The next leg (-p2) picks up from the head and the diary.

Interview forks and transcripts

At eleven interview points a leg ends with a brief, and interview.sh opens a read-only fork of that session. Nothing said there enters the run unless a reply file comes back, which the next leg reads before its first write. interview.sh copies the fork's transcript beside the run, and close.sh copies each leg's transcript into _transcripts/. Session files in the run's config folder are kept for 36,500 days (.claude/settings.json).

What the analyst cannot see before E1

_probe/ and _transcripts/ are closed to the analyst until stage E1, by the permission rules and the sandbox and not only by the stage file. _snap/ holds hashes only and stays open throughout. Until its stage is handed over, the analyst also has no later stage file and no later shelf (stages/, shelves/).
