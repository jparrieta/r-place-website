Organizational forms

Group communities by the changes they asked of the model, explain each group as a candidate form, and audit the whole run.

Input
each community's selected changes to the frozen model 46384686
Output
a tree of communities and six candidate forms, an account of each, the retold model, and an audit
Who acts
the aggregator, Claude Opus 5.5
Contracts
aggregator contract v43, stage cards D4 to D6 and E1 to E2
Dates
24 to 28 October 2026
Counts
558 communities with five or more selected changes clustered; 147 placed beside a similar community; six forms
Cost
42 session hours in 83 sessions; 5,063 million cache-read and 14 million output tokens by the run's counter (run log)

What happens

Communities that asked for similar changes to the model are grouped. The groups are candidate organizational forms: their meaning has to be read from how their communities solved organizing problems, and each form keeps its communities' names, codes and quotations.

How it was done, with r/PlaceNL beside it

  1. Build the tree (D4).

    Ward clustering groups the 558 communities with five or more selected changes. The aggregator chooses the cut into six groups and places the other 147 beside a community it judges similar.

    r/PlaceNL

    r/PlaceNL falls in form B, Behind the thread: chat, scripts, raids: a defining community, fourth nearest of 113 to the form's centre.

  2. Explain the branches (D5).

    An account of each form: what defines it cell by cell, its defining communities, and where they depart from it.

    r/PlaceNL

    r/PlaceNL's 22 changes: 14 restrict, 4 add a condition, 3 add a step, 1 adds a link. Form B's own account calls it a residual grouping, not a form, held together by the chat, the scripts and the raids.

  3. Retell the model (D6).

    The whole model is retold with its forms. New insights stay unvalidated proposals.

  4. Audit (E1) and feedback (E2).

    The analysis and its record are audited, and feedback is used to examine the account.

Strengths and limitations

Strengths

  • Each form is traced to the communities and the return lines that define it.
  • Alternative cuts, at four and eight groups, are recorded beside the chosen six.
  • A form that does not hold together, like B, is labelled as such by its own account.

Limitations

  • The forms are candidates: the cut into six is the aggregator's judgement.
  • Communities with fewer than five changes are placed by similarity, not clustered.
  • No human audit of the forms has been made yet.
