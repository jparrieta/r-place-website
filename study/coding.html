Community coding

Read each community's full record and describe, in its members' words, how it organized for the event.

Input
the community's posts and comments from the opening to a day after the freeze (97 hours), and a brief of its ordinary week before
Output
first-order codes, second-order themes, a case account and a timeline for each community; later a local model (R6)
Who acts
one community coder per community, gpt-5.6-sol at medium effort; fleet coders for communities with more than 500 items; a scheduler that prepares, launches and checks
Contracts
coder contract v32 (v30 for 102 communities), community coder (case lead), fleet coder, scheduler
Dates
7 September to 4 October 2026
Counts
705 communities; 115,388 first-order codes; 7,844 second-order themes
Cost
15.89 billion tokens, cached input included; coding hours were not logged (run log)

What happens

One community coder reads one community's whole record, in time order, and describes what its members did about the event, close to their own words. It sees no other community and no earlier coding. Its account is written before anyone compares communities: the comparison is the next stage's work.

How it was done, with r/PlaceNL beside it

  1. Read the brief.

    A different reader wrote a brief of the community's ordinary week before the event. The coder reads it for orientation and flags any claim without a quote.

    r/PlaceNL

    The brief for r/PlaceNL is empty: the community has no record in the week before. It was founded for the event.

  2. Decide what the case is.

    The coder passes over every thread in time order, twenty-five at a time, and records whether each belongs to the case. Nothing is coded yet. Large communities split here: above 500 items, a fixed gate divides whole threads among fleet coders.

    r/PlaceNL

    701 items in 101 threads. The gate split them into two batches of whole threads, one per fleet coder.

  3. Code in the members' words.

    Each first-order code stays close to how members put it. A code cites the item by reference; the coder never types quotation text.

    r/PlaceNL

  4. Group codes into themes.

    The coder asks of a group of codes: what are these people doing? It names that in their word where they have one. Themes stay descriptive; naming the category above them is the aggregator's job.

    r/PlaceNL

    246 codes on 228 items became 21 themes, from publishing and updating the current plan to fighting among ourselves over whether the Yeti stays.

  5. Write the case account.

    A chronology, then what set off what. A relation needs a stated reason: a reply is only a reply. A counter-reading searches for disconfirming material before the verdict.

    r/PlaceNL

    From the account: The later turn names the same plan and venue, so this is an answered correction rather than a relation inferred from adjacency.

  6. Check before closing.

    The scheduler resolves every cited quote against the record and runs end checks; a failed check sends the work back.

  7. Build a local model (R6).

    Later, the same community coder, resumed with gpt-6.1-sol, builds a standalone model of its own community. The aggregator did not use these models.

    r/PlaceNL

    r/PlaceNL's model explains how members made work possible without control over every Dutch placer: requests gain helpers, neighbours accept reciprocal terms, and obstructed work prompts requests to change the means.

Strengths and limitations

Strengths

  • Each coder reads only its own community, served in order by a tool that logs what it was given; it cannot open the atlas or other coding.
  • Quotations are cited by reference and resolved by script against the record, so no quoted text is typed by an agent.
  • Accounts are written before the population model exists, so the later return can test the model against independent case accounts.

Limitations

  • Quote checks establish that a text occurs in the record, not that it is read correctly.
  • Coordination on Discord and elsewhere is not in the record; r/PlaceNL itself sends members to Discord.
  • In seven communities, single threads of 500 to 5,200 items filled a coder's budget and came back uncoded.
  • One model family coded every community. Coding 300 communities a second time, with another model, will assess variation between models.
